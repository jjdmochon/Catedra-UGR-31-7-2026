# Química Farmacéutica II (QFDOS)

## Overview
Química Farmacéutica II (QFDOS) is a fourth-year course in the Grado en Farmacia at the Universidad de Granada. 

The course shifts focus from general drug design principles to specific therapeutic categories. Students analyze the chemistry, mechanisms of action, and Structure-Activity Relationships (SAR) of major drug classes, including anti-infective agents (antibacterial, antiviral, antiparasitic), cardiovascular drugs, Central Nervous System (CNS) active agents, and anticancer therapeutics.

---

## Core Concepts
- **Structure-Activity Relationships (SAR)**: Systematically identifying how chemical modifications to a core scaffold (e.g., the penam ring or purine nucleus) change biological potency and selectivity.
- **Drug Resistance Mechanisms**: Chemical strategies pathogens and cancer cells use to disable drugs (e.g., beta-lactamase cleavage, active efflux, mutated binding sockets).
- **Bioorthogonal Target Chemistry**: Drugs that exploit unique biochemistry of the pathogen over the human host (e.g., targeting the fungal cell wall or the purine salvage pathway in protozoan parasites).
- **BBB Permeability**: Chemical constraints required for a molecule to cross the blood-brain barrier to target CNS receptors.

## Common Student Misconceptions
- **Misconception: "Antivirals act like antibacterials and destroy the pathogen."**  
  *Correction*: Viruses hijack host cells to replicate. Antivirals are selectively targeted "patch files" that disrupt specific viral replication steps (e.g., viral protease cleavage or RNA-dependent RNA polymerase extension) without halting host transcription/translation.
- **Misconception: "Parasite treatments target the same pathways as bacteria."**  
  *Correction*: Protozoan parasites (like *Trypanosoma cruzi* or *Leishmania*) are eukaryotic. Antibacterial targets (like peptidoglycan synthesis) do not exist. Instead, drugs target specific metabolic differences, such as the parasite’s reliance on host purine salvage pathways. This links to the lab's development of C8-substituted purine derivatives to inhibit parasite enzymes.

## Laboratory & Practical Activities
1. **Synthesis of a Sulfonamide (Sulfanilamide)**: Students perform the synthesis of sulfanilamide from acetanilide, executing crystallization and determining purity via melting point, TLC, and 1H NMR analysis.
2. **Clinical Case Analysis**: Students review structural dossiers of clinical candidates to predict resistance pathways and describe how specific structural modifications (e.g., introducing a fluorine atom to block metabolic oxidation) improve therapeutic efficiency.

---

## Assessment Methods
- **Continuous Evaluation (40%)**: Problem sets matching chemical structures to pharmacological mechanisms, and active participation in UGR continuous evaluation sites.
- **Lab Practical Execution (20%)**: Lab notebook audit, synthesis purity yields, and characterization analysis.
- **Written Final Examination (40%)**: Comprehensive testing of SAR schemes, drug identification by structural features, and synthetic routes for main drug classes.
