# Química Orgánica (QO)

## Overview
Química Orgánica is a foundational second-year course in the Grado en Farmacia at the Universidad de Granada. 

This course introduces students to the syntax of organic chemistry. Students learn to draw, name, and predict the reactivity of carbon-based molecules. Rather than memorizing lists of disjointed reactions, the course teaches students to look at a molecule and immediately locate its electron-rich (nucleophilic) and electron-deficient (electrophilic) centers, allowing them to map out reaction pathways from first principles.

---

## Core Concepts
- **Nucleophile-Electrophile Dynamics**: The driving force of organic chemistry. Reactions are simply the flow of electrons from high density (lone pairs, pi bonds) to low density (polar bonds, positive charges).
- **Stereochemistry**: The 3D orientation of molecules. Chiral centers (sp3 carbons with 4 different groups) create enantiomers—non-superimposable mirror images that can behave as entirely different compounds in biological systems.
- **Reaction Mechanisms**: Step-by-step descriptions of bond breaking and bond forming. Main mechanisms include Nucleophilic Substitution (SN1/SN2), Elimination (E1/E2), Electrophilic Addition, and Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS).
- **Resonance & Electron Delocalization**: How adjacent pi systems distribute electron charge, stabilizing reactive intermediates (carbocations, carbanions, radicals).

## Weekly Syllabus

| Week | Topic | Core Chemical / Computational Analogy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **W1** | Structure and Bonding | Data structures: orbital hybridization and polar covalent bonds. |
| **W2** | Alkanes & Conformational Analysis | Physical states: energy minimization through molecular rotations. |
| **W3** | Stereochemistry & Chirality | Interface orientation: left-hand vs. right-hand molecular signals. |
| **W4** | Nucleophilic Substitution (SN1/SN2) | Data swap: direct displacement vs. intermediate formation. |
| **W5** | Elimination Reactions (E1/E2) | Deprotonation: removing hydrogen to form double-bond conduits. |
| **W6** | Alkenes, Alkynes & Electrophilic Addition | Unlocking pi bonds: using electron clouds to capture electrophiles. |
| **W7** | Aromaticity & EAS | Closed-loop stabilization: substitution on highly stable benzene rings. |
| **W8** | Carbonyl Reactivity & Condensations | Nucleophilic attacks on carbon-oxygen double bonds (carboxylic derivatives). |

## Common Student Misconceptions
- **Misconception: "Curved arrows show the movement of atoms."**  
  *Correction*: Curved arrows in reaction mechanisms show exclusively the movement of **electron pairs**. Atoms migrate only as a consequence of electron pairs forming new bonds or breaking old ones.
- **Misconception: "Enantiomers have identical physical and chemical properties in all environments."**  
  *Correction*: Enantiomers have identical properties *only* in achiral environments. In a chiral biological environment (composed of L-amino acids and D-sugars), enantiomers behave as completely different structures. One hand fits a right-handed glove; the other does not. This is why drug stereochemistry is a critical parameter in medicinal safety (e.g., thalidomide).

## Laboratory & Practical Activities
1. **Basic Operations in Organic Chemistry**: Students practice isolation and purification methods: liquid-liquid extraction, recrystallization, simple/fractional distillation, and thin-layer chromatography (TLC).
2. **Synthesis of an Ester (Esterification)**: Acid-catalyzed condensation of a carboxylic acid and an alcohol (Fischer Esterification), verifying yield and purity.

---

## Assessment Methods
- **Continuous Evaluation (30%)**: Online homework, laboratory notebook reviews, and active participation in workshop classes.
- **Lab Practical Examination (20%)**: Live assessment of synthesis setup, chromatography interpretation, and safety protocols.
- **Written Final Examination (50%)**: Multi-step mechanism predictions, stereochemical assignments, and proposing short synthetic routes to target structures.
