Peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) are a DNA/peptide hybrid consisting of an amide backbone with 2-aminoethyl glycine linkages in place of the phosphodiester DNA backbone, upon which the four nucleobases, adenine (A), cytosine (C), guanine (G), and thymine (T) are displayed.1,2 PNA is a wonderful mimic of DNA, to which it hybridizes following the standard Watson–Crick base pairing,3 with the binding afﬁnity and selectivity of PNA toward DNA being stronger and more discriminative than that between DNA/DNA and DNA/RNA due to the absence of electrostatic repulsion (PNA is non-charged).3 Furthermore, PNA is stable over a wide pH range and is resistant to both nucleases and proteases,4 making PNA a molecule of choice for gene therapy applications, and also for high-throughput analysis us...
