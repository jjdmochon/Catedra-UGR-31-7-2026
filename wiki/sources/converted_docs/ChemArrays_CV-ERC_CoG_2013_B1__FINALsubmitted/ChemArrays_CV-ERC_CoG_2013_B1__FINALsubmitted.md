# **ERC Consolidator Grant 2013 Research proposal Part B1** 

_**(to be evaluated in Step 1)**_ 

“Encoded Chemical Libraries: Creating High-Throughput Cell-based Screening Platforms to Accelerate Drug Discovery” 

## **ChemArrays** 

## Proposal duration: **60 months** 

## Principal Investigator (PI) : **Juan Jose Diaz-Mochon** 

## Host institution: **University of Granada** 

## **Proposal summary** 

This project exploit the power of encoded chemical libraries as a tool for studying phenotypic modifications and cellular differentiation induced by small-molecule for both adherent and suspension cell lines. For the very first time this platform will allow small-molecules to be uptaken by cells in order to induce phenotype changes while still being able to track down each individual molecule cellular impact up to a single cell level. This will be possible by using linkers between small-molecules and their barcode tags which can be broken without the system losing its encoding power.  Two different approaches will be used (i) for adherent cells: a single 2D platform will be converted into a hybrid DNA-polymer-small molecule microarray mixtures where small molecules will be organised spatially in virtue of DNA-PNA hybridization properties. Macromolecular hydrogels will act as mini-wells ,allowing cell based screenings using over 10,000 different compounds in a single experiment. And (ii) for suspension cells: ChemArrays introduce a new concept called ‘one-bead-one-compound & one-cell-one-bead’ (OBOC & OCOB) which will be able to identify chemicals responsible of inducing phenotypic changes into single cells. Therefore this project will deliver a highly innovative technology which will help to find new chemicals and chemical combinations as drugs and also as drivers of cell differentiation. Inducing cellular differentiation by synthetic entities is key to achieve safe and robust cellular therapy products and as this nascent field do not have enough lead compounds to build on them, ChemArrays could be of great for it. ChemArrays project is the bridge which has been missing for 20 years in the field of encoded chemical libraries making possible to use those libraries within complex biological models taking into account dynamic biological processes rather than isolated systems.

## **Section a:** _**Extended Synopsis of the scientific proposal (max. 5 pages)**_ 

## _Description of the proposed work in the context of the state of the art of the field:_ 

Encoded chemical libraries have been validated as an innovative way to greatly accelerate the discovery of interactions between small-molecules and biomolecules of importance such as enzymes and nucleic acids, thus becoming a technology which has attracted increasing interest from biotech and pharmaceutical companies.[1-2] These encoded strategies have been presented as a solution to screen large combinatorial libraries against biomolecules in a meaningful manner and offering a second chance to combinatorial approaches. Once, combinatorial chemistry was hailed as the future for drug discovery due to their ability of generating large number of compounds in relatively short period of time, however those great hopes were never met as they could not generate many meaningful biological data. The difficulty to screen large libraries of compounds within biological systems could not be initially overcome but encoding approaches are changing this perception. For the encoding task, DNA and Peptide Nucleic Acids (PNA) have already being used as barcode tags for a variety of applications, most notably the elegant work of David Liu in Harvard [3] and Nicolas Winssinger [4] in Strasbourg and also by myself together with Mark Bradley in Edinburgh [5]. During the past 8 years dozens of large DNA encoded small-molecules libraries have been synthesised by big institutions such as Standford, ETH Zurich and Harvard and also big pharma companies such as  GSK using split-and-mix approaches, which allow synthesising a number of compounds which increase exponentially with the number of chemical steps and therefore reducing massively time and materials required. [1, 6-8] However to date it has not been possible to test the effects of encoded small molecules on cellular phenotype in a microarray format. The latest innovation in this field has presented the use of PNA encoded peptide libraries with cellular systems such lymphocytes and adherent cell lines to find peptides as selective cellular penetrating peptides [9] and integrin ligands [10], an innovation of which I have been a key part of it. 

While those latest results show the feasibility of using these libraries with more complex systems it does still not resolve the issue of how release encoded small-molecules within cells to induce phenotypic changes and then unequivocally identified which small-molecules are responsible of those changes. In order to truly be considered a proper platform based on large library of compounds for accelerating drug discovery and also finding chemicals which induce cellular differentiation the previous issue has to be resolved for both suspension and adherent lines (e.g. stem cells, primary cells, suspension, cancer stem cells, circulating tumoral). This proposal presents key innovative steps to allow the use of large encoded libraries as chemical tools to create High-Throughput Cell-based Phenotypic Screening Platforms. 

A key issue to be resolved is how to cleave small-molecule compounds from their tags and still be able to track down that small-molecule after causing a phenotypic change in the cell. ChemArrays offer two different approaches depending on cell type. 

(i) _Adherent cells._ In this part, split and mix libraries having both PNA and DNA barcode tags will be synthesised on solid support, following established protocols. [1, 10] Two libraries based on triazine and adenine scaffolds will be initially prepared (see **Scheme 1** ). The key issue will be resolved by using (i)

(ii) _For suspension cells._ In this case, a different and more challengeable approach has to be followed. A perfect assay could be named as one-bead-one-compound & one-cell-one-bead (OBOC & OCOB) as it would need to be able to have just one encoded small-molecule per cell and be released intracellularly Then, following immunostaining, cells could be sorted by fluorescence-acting cell sorting (FACS) based on phenotypic changes and, at that stage, still be able to track down which small-molecules are responsible of certain phenotypic changes (see **Figure 2** ). Using FACS gating, that population could then be clearly identified thanks to use fluorescent microspheres with a fluorophore different to the immunostainning reagents. These assays will be performed at GENyO which is equipped with last generation FACS facilities. ## _Ground-breaking nature of the research project :_ 

This project will deliver a highly innovative technology which will help to find new chemicals and chemical combinations as drugs and also as driver of cell differentiation and therefore direct applicable to cell therapy at a speed not seen before. Inducing cellular diferentiation by synthetic entities is key to achieve safe and robust cellular therapy products and this nascent field do not have of enough lead compounds to build on them. ChemArrays could thus be of great help to find new chemical entities.Apart from that, ChemArrays will become a useful tool to develop patient-based cancer drugs helping to asses rapidly drug efficacy using primary tumoral cell lines. Its unique features, automation, multiplexing and low amount of cells required to identify small molecules that target those cell lines, especially hard to drug cell lines, such as cancer stem cells could mean a critical shift in the field of personalised medicine. Finally, this technology will also be valuable for chemical genetic screens. Chemical genetics involves testing thousands of small molecules for their effects on a cellular or organismal phenotype; subsequently, the proteins targeted by these phenotypemodifying compounds are identified. 

The development of such an exciting and innovative technology may provide opportunities to engage with European and multinational companies specialised in drug discovery and as well as potentially providing new market opportunities to develop new platforms to support the exploitation of the technology.

## _Overall aims and objectives of the research programme:_ 

_(a) Development of a HTS methodology to analyse arrays of metal-grafted hydrogels for cellular attachment while allowing controlled release of small molecules._ 

_(b) Synthesis of split and mix DNA and PNA-encoded libraries of small-molecules using solid phase chemistry._ 

_(c) Spatial organization of the libraries in a 2D format using DNA microarrays, based on PNA-DNA hybridization properties_ 

_(d) Production of DNA-small molecules-polymer hybrid arrays.. This will allow cleaving of small-molecules from barcode tags while embed in the polymer spots. Polymer spots will act as mini-wells where cells will be adhered and small-molecules released._ 

_(e) Use of hybrid arrays to carry out cell-based assays to study phenotype changes in cancer cell lines, including cancer stem cells, and differentiation process in pluripotent cell cultures (i.e., embryonic carcinoma) that can be obtained from commercial sources._ 

_(f) Synthesis of a PNA-encoded library of small-molecules on polystyrene-microspheres._ 

_(g) Use of the PS-microsphere supported PNA-encoded library to study phenotype changes in human immortalised myelogenous leukemia cell line K562 assisted by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS)_
