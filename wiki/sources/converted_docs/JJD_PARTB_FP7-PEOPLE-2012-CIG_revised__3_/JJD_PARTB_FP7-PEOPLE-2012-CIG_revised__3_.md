## **B 1.1.Scientific and technological Quality, including any interdisciplinary and multidisciplinaryaspects of the proposal** 

**Project Aims:** The aim of the proposed research is to design novel reagents and tools for the detection and quantification of microRNAs (miRNAs) with a particular focus on miRNA species involved in solid tumours and hematologic cancers. The proposed project will thus provide a new chemical toolbox which will allow the rapid detection of miRNAs involved in cancers. This proposal intends to take advantage of the novel dynamic chemistry approach to profile directly nucleic acids and develop based on it novel tools to provide simple, sensitive, rapid, multiplexed assays to profile miRNAs. The longer-term vision is that this dynamic chemistry approach will enable clinicians to be able to rapidly and accurately diagnose and prognosis cancer by combining the precision of the high affinity molecular probes and novel set of biomarkers based on miRNAs. Therefore, the proposed project is positioned at the interface of chemistry, biology and pre-clinical medicine with three main aims: 

_(i) Design, synthesis and validation of chemical reagent sets for novel cost-effective miRNA profiling assays._ 

_(ii) Development of microsphere miRNA-capture devices that will allow monitoring of miRNAs directly from body fluids._ 

_(iii) Development of microarray-based detection method “miOncochip”, for oncomiRs detection._ 

## **Background of the State of the Art of Research in the Field** 

During the past decade microRNAs (a novel species of non-coding RNAs with gene regulatory function) have been linked virtually to all known physiological and pathological processes, including cancer.[1-3] In the same way as certain key protein-coding genes, miRNAs can be deregulated in cancer, in which they can function as a group to mark differentiation states (cancer classifiers)or individually as real oncogenes or tumour suppressors (cancer genes).[4 ] Understanding the role of miRNA in biological processes is thus key to investigate cancer phenotypes, diagnose and classified cancer types in a much stratified manner and also to develop new therapies which either target or mimics miRNAs. Components of the miRNA machinery and miRNAs themselves are involved in many cellular processes that are altered in cancer, such as differentiation, proliferation and apoptosis.[5 ] _In vitro_ studies have showed evidence implicating miRNAs such as miR-21 and miR-10 in cancer.[6] For instance, in miRNA profiling experiments, miR-21 is found to be overexpressed in most of the tumour types examined so far, including neuroblastoma, glioblastoma, colorectal, lung, breast, pancreas, leukaemia and lymphoma.[7-10] Recently, Medina _et al_ have studied the profile of miRNA expression in 540 human samples for six tumour types, miR21 was the only miRNA upregulated in all types.[6, 11 ] To analyse miRNA scientist adapted tools which are use for the majority of DNA genotyping methods at present relying upon DNA polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for the exponential amplification of DNA target molecules. Therefore, most of the efforts to do miRNA analysis have been based on PCR technologies to create cDNA from miRNA. However this approach is particularly challenging to miRNA short nature (2224 nucleotides). This means that standard protocols for miRNA analysis are long and still inefficient (to date a few reports comparing different platforms cannot find very good reproducibility between them), including a ligation step to elongate the short RNA sequences reverse transcription and PCR to provide a product, cDNA, that can be analysed either via qRTPCR or arrays (surfaces or beads).[12-13] Due to the striking role of miRNA in cancer - it is commonly accepted that the widespread alteration in the expression of miRNAs is an omnipresent feature of cancer-most of the biological assay providers have entered into this market. _B1.2.3Novel miOncochip for oncomiRs detection_ 

The final aim of this project is to develop an efficient microarray-based detection platform, socalled “miOncochip”, for detection of miRNAs associated to cancer. The concept introduced here will be a combination of the standard microarray approaches in association with the chemical-based technology for nucleic acid testing to allow detection and quantification of miRNAs with improved speed of analysis and absence of false positives. 

The core principle behind microarrays is the hybridization between two DNA strands, the property of complementary nucleic acid sequences to specifically pair with each other by forming hydrogen bonds between complementary nucleotide base pairs. After washing-off of non-specific bonding sequences, only strongly paired strands will remain hybridized. So fluorescently labelled target sequences that bind to a probe sequence generate a signal.[30] However, these technologies require complex and expensive processes for targeted nucleic acid labelling, especially in the case of miRNA,and a very fine temperature control to avoid mis-hybridization which give rise to false positives as just a single hybridization step is responsible of the signal. This is particularly difficult when high homology sequences have to be analysed. It would be exceptionally useful to be able to miniaturize oncomiRs detection in devices known as biochips, including microarrays and lab-on-achip (LoC) microfluidic devices, such as the In-Check Platform from ST Microelectronics, without the risk of false positives and being able to use label-free miRNAs. The proposed project offer such solution.

## **B1.3 Originality and innovative nature of the project, and relationship to the 'state of the art' of research in the field** 

The core technology, invented by the candidate and Prof. Bradley (PCT/GB2008/003185) allows a direct read-out of short nucleotides sequences without need for additional probe/target recognition, PCR amplification or labelling of the targeted nucleic acid, making it ideal for miRNA profiling. The technology has several important features including the ability to eliminate false positive results (as there is a thermodynamic process which control the nucleobase recognition and therefore avoiding the mis-incorporation), which are a common feature of other detection technologies (eg PCR and arrays). The technology shows both high specificity and sensitivity, and is a highly novel and innovative technique. To date the technology has been validated for cystic fibrosis genotyping using mass spectrometry, MALDI-ToF, to detect the assay signal and outcome. A potential limitation to the widespread adoption is that mass spectrometry is required in order to read the signal or assay output. Therefore, this project propose a key innovative step to apply fluorescence detection methods in order to make the assay cheap and easy to perform without the use of expensive and specialist equipment such as  MALDI-TOF. This innovation will greatly facilitate the integration of the technology within state of the art platforms providing them with an unique way of solving traditional problems associated to microarray an bead based detection systems. The new platforms will be the very first one which are not based on just hybridization process but also on a second step which will eliminate the risk of false positives.
