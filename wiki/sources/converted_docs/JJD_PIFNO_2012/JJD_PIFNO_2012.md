## **1. SUMMARY OF THE PROPOSAL** (the summary must be also filled in Spanish) 

PROJECT TITLE: Integrating Nanotechnology with Dynamic Chemistry for Fluorescence in-situ micro-RNA Analysis. High Specific and Sensitive Multiplex Platform 

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR: Juan J. Díaz Mochón 

SUMMARY (brief and precise, outlining only the most relevant topics and the proposed objectives) 

The aim of the project is to develop novel reagents (fluorescently-labelled abasic peptide nucleic acid – PNA- probes and aldehyde-modified nucleobases) and protocols to perform fluorescence in-situ hybridization assays for the simultaneous detection of RNA. Of particular interest is the possibility of performing micro-RNA in-situ hybridization (ISH) assays which are very challenging for the current ISH technologies due to their short length (20-26nt). The proposal takes advantage of the very high **specificity** given by an already validated chemical-based technology for nucleic acid detection together with the high **sensitivity** offered by fluorescently labelled-nanoparticles. Biorthogonal groups will be attached to the aldehyde-modified nucleobases and will act as handle for nanoparticles modified by specific partners of each bio-orthogonal tag. In this way, fluorescence nanoparticles will be the signal “amplifiers”providing the required sensitivity to the process. This proposal will tackle the intrinsic issue of many of the current ISH technologies which relates to false positives created by signals coming from unbound-probes, labels and/or mismatched hybridizations. 

To avoid false-positives two different approaches will be investigated (i) signal co-localization and (ii) FRET. The chemical-based approach specifically allows the single dynamic incorporation of an aldehyde-modified tagged-nucleobases on the abasic PNA when the target RNA strand templates the reaction. Therefore, only when both entities, labelled-PNA probes and nucleobases, will be in close proximity a positive signal through either co-localization or FRET will be generated. This unique and innovative chemical-based technology will avoid false-positives and therefore will become a very exciting prospect as a clinical diagnostic tool.

TÍTULO DEL PROYECTO: Integración de Nanotecnología con Química Dinámica para el Análisis Fluorescente In Situ de microRNA. Plataforma Multiplex de Alta Especificidad y Sensibilidad 

RESUMEN (breve y preciso, exponiendo solo los aspectos más relevantes y los objetivos propuestos) 

El objetivo de este proyecto es el desarrollo de nuevos reactivos y protocolos para realizar ensayos de hibridación fluorescente in situ para la detección simultánea de RNA, mRNA y micro-RNA. Es especialmente interesante la posibilidad de realizar ensayos de fluorescencia in situ de micro-RNA debido a que son muy difíciles de detectar con las tecnologías actuales debido a la corta longitud de dichos oligonucleótidos (20-26nt). 

Esta propuesta presenta ventajas únicas que provienen de dos factores, la alta **especificidad** de la tecnología de análisis de ácidos nucleicos basada en química dinámica conjuntamente con la alta **sensibilidad** que ofrecen las nanopartículas fluorescentes. Para conseguir que ambas tecnologías puedan funcionar conjuntamente, las nucleobases funcionalizadas con aldehidos que se usan en la química dinámica se modificarán con grupos biortogonales para que éstos puedan ser reconocidos de forma muy específica por sus correspondientes partner reactivos, que se encontrarán en la superficie de las nanopartículas. De este modo las nanopartículas fluorescentes actúan como amplificadores de señales. Esta propuesta aborda uno de los problemas intrínsecos de muchas de las tecnologías actuales, la presencia de falsos positivos creados por señales procedentes de sondas no hibridizadas, etiquetas sueltas y/o mis-hibridaciones. 

Para evitar los falsos positivos se van a llevar a cabo dos estrategias diferentes: (i) señales colocalizadas, y (ii) FRET. La estrategia basada en el reconocimiento de ácidos nucleicos por medio de química dinámica permite la incorporación selectiva de las nucleobases-aldehído a los oligómeros abásicos de PNA marcados con marcadores fluorescentes, sólo cuando la hebra de RNA complementaria está presente y por tanto moldea la reacción de incorporación. Por lo tanto, sólo cuando ambas entidades, las sondas de PNA y la nucleobase incorporada estén muy próximas una señal positiva se generará mediante colocalización o  FRET. Esta tecnología basada en hibridazión y química dinámica será única e innovadora ya que evitará los falsos positivos y, por lo tanto, se convertirá en una herramienta muy interesante en el campo del diagnóstico clínico.

## 2.1 Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) 

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) has been used for more than two decades since early 1980.[1] Those probes have been mainly used to detect, and localize the presence or absence of specific DNA sequences on chromosomes by fluorescence microscopy.[2] FISH uses fluorescent probes that bind to only those parts of the chromosome with which they show a high degree of sequence complementarity. FISH is often used for finding specific features in DNA and it is use by health services to give genetic counselling[3] and as a tool of molecular diagnosis (Fig.1).[4,5] 

FISH probes have also been developed to detect RNAs within tissue samples. While whole gene expression profiles „paint a picture‟ of a tissue as if it were homogeneous, for cases as tumoral tissues where heterogeneity is a prevalent feature FISH technologies help to define the spatial-temporal patterns of gene expression within cells and tissues.[6] The principle of any FISH RNA assays is based on the use of probes with high specificity towards the target strand which provide enough sensitivity meaning high signal strengths from attaching the target RNA with so many fluorescent dye molecules that each RNA molecule becomes visible as a diffraction-limited fluorescent spot. It is assumed that generally 30–50 labels are needed to detect individual molecules of mRNA.[6] Three different approaches have been developed (i) to use a large number of fluorescently-labelled oligonucleotides which target the same RNA target[7,][8] , (Fig. 2) (ii) to use less number of probes but heavily labelled[9] and (iii) to use a sandwich approach to amplify the signal through multiple hybridizations (Fig. 3).[10  ]

## 2.2. micro-RNA 

In the past decade miRNAs have emerged as regulators of gene expression that act as markers of tissues and pathological conditions.[13a] Despite rapid advances in our understanding of miRNA biogenesis and mechanism,[13] many fundamental questions still remain regarding miRNA function and their influence on cell cycle control. Their role have started to be decoded and have been presented as “braker”/”accelerator” of cell biology through regulation of mRNA translation.[14] Therefore they have been postulated as key players in diseases such as cancer.[15] They have been identified in tissues by rtPCR and can discriminate normal from tumour tissue. Considering recent reports and the heterogeneity found within cancer tissues it is likely that looking at the average miRNA expression of cell populations could result in the loss of important information connecting miRNA expression and cell function. Therefore in situ miRNAs detection in biopsy material would add a new avenue of evidence for diagnosis and prognosis. The main challenge in FISH applications is the optimization of sensitivity and specificity during probe design. This is even more acute when miRNA have to be detected due to their short length. Currently, there are no many efficient techniques to quantify miRNA expression at the single-cell level. 

There are a few reports which present ISH technologies for miRNA analysis.[16 ] These reports used tagged-LNA which allowed enzyme-based signal amplification.[16,17] By tagging their probes with bio-orthogonal tags such as fluorescein, biotin or digogexin, which are not normally found within cells, “ELISA” type assays were able to be performed in fixed tissues, providing fluorescence signals where miRNAs were found. The big issue of these approaches is that they rely on a single hybridizaton step without being able to differentiate between signals coming from hybridised and non-hybridised probes, meaning that it will be difficult to avoid false-positive results. 

## 2.3. Bio-orthogonal tags (BOT) 

More and more often biomolecules, which can be either exogenous or created by the cell's own biosynthetic machinery through genetic engineering, are used to monitor and track different molecular biology processes. Therefore, there was a great need to tag these entities with groups which are non-native chemical handles that can be recognised through highly selective reactions by exogenously delivered probes. The

chemical biology field provided an answer to that need via the bio-orthogonal chemical tags (BOT).[18] Generally speaking, the bioorthogonal chemical reporter strategy involves the incorporation of unique functionality into targets of interest, followed by chemical labelling with a small-molecule probe. Bertozzi described this concept as follow _“the selective reaction of compounds A and B bearing bioorthogonal functional groups proceeds in the presence of all the functionality found within living systems”.[19]_ A recent application which offers the amplification of biomarker signals using the bio-orthogonal concept has been presented by Weissleder.[20] They modified antibodies with a bioorthogonal chemical tag - trans-cyclooctene- which were then labelled by magnetofluorescent nanoparticles coated with tetrazine through a metal-free chemoselective cycloaddition in living cells. They showed that the technique, named BOND (bioorthogonal nanoparticle detection), was fast, chemoselective and adaptable to metal nanomaterials and capable to profile cancer cells using diagnostic magnetic resonance. This is a great example of how by integrating different chemical biology tools it is possible to create new technologies which can be applied for diagnosis purposes. 

## 2.4 Nanoparticles 

Nanoparticles-based assays offer a powerful approach for any aspects of drug discovery, encoding and diagnostic in medicine.[29] Fluorescent particles are used for a diverse number of biochemical assays including intracellular imaging, cellular tracking, as well as detection of a variety of biomolecules. Many strategies can be carried out for labeling of nanoparticles such as  incorporation of the fluorescent dyes during polymerization process (that has the potential to generate highly fluorescent particles without reduction in the levels of functional groups available for conjugation of a desired cargo (drug, ligand, protein, etc) or employment of fluorescent monomers (that has the advantage of generating a  homogeneous fluorescence distribution and long term fluorescence stability).[30] These which have been used include rhodamine and fluorescein that have been conjugated to an acrylic acid unit[31] or nitrooxabenzodiazole linked to a methylmethacrylate and vinyl-derviatized naphthalimide.[32] 

## 2.5. Non-enzymatic Nucleic Acid Analysis 

Several non-enzymatic based methods, apart from the classic allele-specific oligonucleotide (ASO) technology which is used for doing in situ hybridization, for the detection of nucleic acid analysis have been reported. DNA mimics such as peptide nucleic acids (PNAs)[21] have been used in a number of ligation-based chemical approaches, most notably in the elegant work of Seitz.[22] Nonenzymatic ligation has also been achieved in a DNA–DNA sense by Kool[23] who ligated DNA strands containing a 3‟-phosphorothioate with a 5‟-iodothymidine, and Richert[24] who reacted nucleotides possessing an activated phosphate with a DNA strand containing a free 3‟- amino group. DNA-templated dynamic chemistry has attracted interest for the preparation of stimuli-responsive polymers and for gaining insight into the chemistry of primordial selfreplicating systems. Most recently, Liu and Heemstra have reported PNA-templated base-filling reactions on PNA strands.[25 ] However it is just us who have reported a distinct enzyme-free, dynamic chemistry based method of producing allele specific products for genotyping (see Section 3.2 and 4.1 for chemical structures).[26, 27, 28]

## **3.1** Describe the reasons to present this proposal and the **initial hypothesis** which support its objectives (maximum **20** lines) 

Micro-RNAs are key regulators of translational of an organism being responsible of different pathologies such as cancer, i.e. miRNA-21 it is over-expressed in nearly all solid tumours. Therefore they comprise a new class of promising diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers. 

New probes and protocols for microRNA analysis in tissues need to be developed to be used as clinical diagnostic tools. Current in-situ hybridization technologies use probes to multi-label, via selective hybridization, either DNA fragments or mRNA strands. However and due to the short lenght of microRNA these approaches based on hybridization are very challenging for analysing them as there is just one hybridization step which control the whole outcome of the assay thereby being very prone to give false-positives. 

Herein, we present a chemical based approach which is partially based on hybridization technologies. Most importantly, it offers a key innovation by introducing a proof reading step through dynamic chemistry giving rise to a unique way to control false-positive not just for miRNA detection but for any other nucleic acid. This unique two step process for "reading“ short micro-RNA produce two fluorescence signals in close proximity ONLY when a target nucleic acid is present, removing problems associated to false positives. 

While the specificity of the new technology is controlled by the already validated dynamic chemical approach the sensitivity will be achieved by using fluorescently-labelled nanoparticles. In order to interface both an elegant and precise chemical biology tool, bio-orthogonality, will be used. Bioorthogonal tags (BOT) and their reactive counter partner will be add to both modified nucleobases and nano-particles respectively so the labelling process can occur efficiently.
