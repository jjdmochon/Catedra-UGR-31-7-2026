## **7. Project abstract** (maximum400 words) 

Genetic profiling of solid tumours is currently undertaken using surgical or biopsy specimens. However, biopsies due to their invasive nature cannot always beperformed routinely owing to their invasive nature and patient fragility. A single biopsy while providing a spatiallyand temporally limited snap-shot of a tumour can fail to reflect its heterogeneity, and lead to errors regarding patient treatment. A liquid biopsy, in this case blood sample, though, not only has the power tomap the genetic landscape of all tumour lesions (primary and metastases),but very valuably opens up the systematic tracking of genomic evolution and with this, the earliest diagnosis of treatment resistance and the real time monitoring. Liquid Biopsy (LB)has now been verified with prognostic markers for disease progression in various cancer types, specially in NSCLC. The gold standard achievement of LB is ‘Precision Medicine’, where differences foundamong people of different racial, ethnic and familial backgrounds enable accurate identification of cancer risk, incidence and survival, and improved treatment decisions. LB overcomes a very significant challenge for clinicians treating lung cancer patients, the lack of tissue available for monitoring a patient over time. 

This project has the clear aims of, primarily,(i)validation of the genetic druggable alterations present in circulating tumour cells (CTCs),RNA exosomal and free DNA isolates from patients’ blood and (ii) validation of the prognostic value of genetic alterations detected on the same components for relapse. Secondarily,looking at thecorrelation of the genetic profiles with the clinical-pathological characteristics observed, referencing standard biomarkersused in NSCLC and clinical outcome, to identify the best prognostic value. 

The systematic approach presented here will validate the effect of the genetic alterations and the different allelic frequenciesfound among different races as novel biomarkers for predicting clinical outcome in NSCLC patients. With LBs being able to provide repeat ‘low invasive’ biopsies readily and tracking of patient genetic analyses over time with earlier assessment, thiswill improve patient risk assessment and adjustment of treatments. Outcome for health services will be also improved as the stratification of NSCLC patientsimproveclinical decisions and interventions. When these are adjusted to the individual risk of each cancer patient the patient outcomes will be improved their quality of life and life expectancies.

## **9. Project description** (maximum 3,000 words) 

## **a. Description of the project: medical need** 

Lung cancer (LC) remains a very significant cause of cancer-related deaths, with over 1 million deceased reported worldwide every year, the overall survival rate for patients with LC below 15%. Despite advances in treatment and diagnosis, it still has a very poor prognosis. It has been reported that around 60% of lung adenocarcinomas carry oncogenic driver mutations. The study of these mutations has provided new drug targets and novel drugs (1). 

One of the main targets is the **Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor** (EGFR) and its signaling pathways, which are well known to be actively involved in NSCLC disease progression through angiogenesis, cell survival, proliferation and metastasis. EGFR mutations are the most frequent driver mutation in NSCLC; it has been reported to be present in 40-60% Asian, 15-20% Caucasian and around 30% Latin-American patients. This discovery has led the development of targeted therapies against the EGFR mutated form, the tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as gefitinib, erlotinib or afatinib(1). 

Other mutations have now been identified as valuable in targeted therapiesin NSCLC. One of these is the **ELM4-ALK** fusion gene, detected in 3-8% of NSCLC patients (1). This mutation leads to the synthesis of a fusion protein which induces a constitutive activation of the ALK receptor and consequently its downstream pathway. The discovery of this translocation led to the development of the ALK inhibitor crizotinib, approvedfor use in advanced NSCLC ALK+ patients. Other potential targets for directed therapy include point mutations in **BRAF** , **K-RAS, ROS1, PIK3CA** or **NTRK1** genes.

The, detection of oncogenic driver mutations is mostly identified through tumour tissue based tests, using techniques such as immunohistochemistry (IHC), fluorescent hybridization in situ (FISH) and more recently, next generation sequencing (NGS). These techniques, although useful, still depend on there being sufficient tumour tissue recovered in the biopsy, where a tumour biopsy has been performed. Moreover, the tumour biopsy taken cannot represent the whole molecular profiling of the tumour. Targeted therapies in NSCLC will greatly benefit such patients, if only earlier diagnosis and more sensitive detections of oncogenic driver mutations could be detected, and then monitored over time as the treatment progresses. This would allow to anticipate clinical and radiological progression detection improving the treatment choice. 

It has been recognised now for some years that the problems noted above could be potentially solved thanks to testing Liquid Biopsies’ (LB).In conjunction with new detection technologies, clinically relevant information from these **(LB)** has created enormous interest (3). The LB concept promises to reveal clinically relevant information, in a non-invasive fashion, analysing blood, saliva or other body fluids of cancer patients. 

Tested so far in liquid biopsies are circulating tumour cells (CTCs), circulating-tumour DNA (ctDNA) and exosomes (for tumour exosomal nucleic acid). They can be a reliable and trustworthy source of information about tumour heterogeneity, treatment response and early stage detection. 

_**CTCs**_ (3) Circulating tumour cells (CTCs) are cells that have separated from the cancer primary site or metastatic, and disseminate into the peripheral blood circulation. CTCs are understood to be part of the long process of cancer metastasis. It is well established that LC releases only a limited number of CTCs. This makes their analysis difficult, due topoor methods of isolation. However, thanks to the improvement in knowledge about CTCs, detection and isolation methods. In general, the value of studies of CTCs in solid tumours include (a) risk prediction for metastatic progression and relapse, (b) staging determination of success and real-time observation of responses to therapies, (c) determination of therapeutic targets and mechanisms of resistance and (d) understanding and tracking the metastasis process in solid tumour patients. _**The ctDNA**_ (3) is released into the blood stream as a result of apoptotic or necrotic processes in the tumoral cells. This is increased in cancer patients due to both a higher replication rate of the tumour cells and the ineffective removal of apoptotic and tumour cells, resulting in higher levels of ctDNA in the peripheral blood. Thanks to new analysis techniques such as digital droplet PCR (ddPCR) and NGS, it is now possible to detect molecular alterations with clinical value. In 2016, the FDA approved a companion diagnostic blood test to detect the EGFR mutation in ctDNA for treatment of NSCLC patients with erlotinib. Other alterations have been described in ctDNA such as, KRAS (1). 

**The exosomes** (3) Exosomes are a class of extracellular vesicles released by all cells, with a size range of 40-150 nm and a lipid bilayer membrane. Exosomes contain DNA, RNA, and proteins. Exosomesare found in significant numbers in body fluids including the blood. Additionally, they may mediate specific cell-to-cell communication and activate signalling pathways in cells they fuse or interact with. Exosomes are detected in the tumor microenvironment, and emerging evidence suggests that they play a role in facilitating tumorigenesis by regulating angiogenesis, immunity, and metastasis. 

CTCs, circulating ctDNA and exosomes (LB) could identify their risk for metastatic relapse or metastatic progression. Moreover, these components in NSCLCpatients could display a set of genetic and epigenetic differences common for tumours including oncogene activation, chromosome loss, and tumour-suppressor gene silencing by methylation.

## **c. Statement of the research hypothesis** 

Circulating biomarkers, such as circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) circulating tumour cells (CTCs) and exosomes are expected to significantly enhance our knowledge about the metastatic process in NSCLC. Differences in cancer risk, incidence and survival among people occur due to different racial and ethnic.The detection of the allelic frequencies in different populations, will enhance health services abilities to predict not only the cancer risk more accurately but also the evolution of disease (5). Better and earlier risk stratification of patients will produce improved patient outcomes in quality of life and life expectancies. 

Additionally, the improved genetic analysis based on liquid biopsies and analysing multiparametricclinically valuable biomarkers will improve treatment options by focusing on the very cells that are the direct precursors of distant metastatic disease, and inherently different than the primary tumour's biology. 

To shift the current clinical paradigm assessing tumour biology in real time by molecular profiling of LB will serve to predict treatment response, monitor patients for emergence of drug and treatment resistances, and may even assist in the identification of novel therapeutic targets.

## **1)CTCs analysis** 

10 ml extraction of blood will be stored in STRECK specific tubes. ## **2) cfDNA analysis** 

10 ml of blood samples will be stored in Citrate specific tubes. The blood sample will be processed using one step of centrifugation to separation of plasma. Then, el plasma will be stored at −80°C until use **.** The Extraction of cfDNA will be performed usingDNeasy blood kit (Qiagen)/ QIAamp Circulating Nucleic Acid kit (QIAGEN) according to  the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA will be quantified with the Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer and Qubit dsDNA HS Assay kit (Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA) according to the recommended protocol.Finally,cfDNA target mutations detected by IonChef Sequencing and 316 PGM chip v 2 (ThermoFisher) **.**

10ml of blood will be stored in EDTA tubes.Isolation of exosomal RNA from plasma through commercial kit (ExoRNeasy mini kit, Qiagen©) and quantization by Nanodrop® (Thermo Scientific©). The RNA collected will be used for the NGS analysis of the mutations and the EML4-ALK transcription. For isolation of exosomes a commercial kit (ExoEasy kit, Qiagen©) will be used.

## **f) Novelty and originality of the project** 

Our proposed LiquidLung will constitute the first study that will compare and validate the prognostic value of CTCs/cfDNA and exosomes in an multicentric work to identify the relevance of the “liquid biopsy”to personalized cancer medicine **.** While there are other studies where these biomarkers have been analysed individually, this study will be the first where the three are done simultaneously, hence increasing the power of the analysis. On top of that, this study will be able to identify different allelic frequencies found among different races, a key element to predict clinical outcome in NSCLC patients.
